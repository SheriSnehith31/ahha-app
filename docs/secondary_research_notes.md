# Secondary Research — Blind/Low-Vision Creator Perspectives

Web research pass (not a substitute for the Tier 2 session — intended to
inform question design and give the wording A/B tests a literature-backed
hypothesis to test against).

## Directly relevant to the left/center/right vs. clock-position A/B test

Found real prior-art disagreement on exactly the question the Tier 2 session
is designed to test:

- Clock-position phrasing ("hazard at your 2 o'clock") is standard in O&M
  training and works well for users taught it — but multiple sources note
  younger low-vision users without daily exposure to analog clock faces find
  it confusing, and users commonly report difficulty distinguishing adjacent
  positions (e.g., 1 vs. 2 o'clock).
- Plain left/right/center laterality alone has been reported as
  "insufficient" in at least one navigation-system usability study —
  users wanted more precision than three coarse buckets gave them.
- Net implication for your test: **expect a generational/O&M-training split**
  in preference, not a clean winner. Worth adding an "have you had formal
  O&M training?" screening question to the Tier 2 protocol so you can
  segment the A/B results by that variable instead of averaging over a
  possibly bimodal preference.

Source: Wayfindr design-standard research and a ScienceDirect usability study
on instruction modality for visually-impaired navigation assistants
(aggregated via web search; recommend pulling the primary sources directly —
[sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581923001076](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581923001076) and
[wayfindr.net/open-standard/designing-for-vision-impaired-people/design-principles](https://www.wayfindr.net/open-standard/designing-for-vision-impaired-people/design-principles)
— before citing in the final report, since these are secondhand summaries).

## General navigation pain points (creator interviews/public statements)

- **Molly Burke**: has publicly described tripping down stairs and injuring
  an ankle while using her cane — a real-world instance of exactly the
  failure mode AHHA's Critical tier targets (stairs_down). Also emphasizes
  independence and the psychological cost of disorientation, which supports
  weighting trust/confidence metrics as seriously as raw accuracy in your
  evaluation protocol. ([blindabilities.com transcript](https://blindabilities.com/?p=2941), [glidance.io profile](https://www.glidance.io/news/glider-spotlight-meet-molly-burke-blind-influencer-unseen))
- **Tommy Edison**: content focuses more on everyday misidentification humor
  than navigation-specific pain points in what surfaced; less directly
  useful for wording decisions than Burke's material. ([tommyedison.com](https://tommyedison.com/))
- **Lucy Edwards**: search did not surface specific statements on navigation
  app language preferences — the general assistive-navigation landscape
  (BlindSquare, Soundscape, NaviLens) came up instead, which is more useful
  as competitive-analysis context than as a language-preference source.

## Caveat

This is a shallow web pass, not a transcript review of actual videos —
treat it as a hypothesis generator for your Tier 2 question design (e.g.
the O&M-training screening question above), not as a citable finding on its
own. The primary research is still the Tier 2 session itself.
